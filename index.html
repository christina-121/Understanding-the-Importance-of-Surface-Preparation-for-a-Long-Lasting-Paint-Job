🛠️🧹 surface preparation

clean · sand · patch · caulk · prime · why it matters

The secret to a paint job that looks great and lasts for years isn't in the can — it's in the prep. Before a single drop of color touches your walls, professionals spend time cleaning, sanding, patching, caulking, and priming. 🧼 clean & sand: creating the perfect canvas

Cleaning is non‑negotiable. Kitchens have grease, bathrooms have soap scum, and even living rooms accumulate dust. A simple wipe with a damp rag isn't enough — pros use trisodium phosphate (TSP) or a substitute, then rinse thoroughly. Sanding follows: it smooths patches and deglosses old paint. For walls, 120‑150 grit is typical; for trim, 150‑180. Always sand in the direction of the surface, and wipe away dust with a tack cloth.

🔨 patching & caulking: details that matter

Small holes and dents are filled with spackle (for small spots) or joint compound (for larger areas). Apply with a putty knife, let dry, then sand flush. Caulking is the final detail: run a thin bead along trim/wall intersections, baseboards, and window/door casings. Tool it with a wet finger or tool for a smooth finish. This step alone makes rooms look crisp and professionally finished.

🎨🧪 primer: the unsung hero

Primer isn't just "thinned‑down paint." It serves specific purposes:

  • stain blocking: covers water stains, smoke, marker
  • adhesion: bonds to glossy or challenging surfaces
  • uniformity: prevents "flashing" (uneven sheen) when painting over patches or different materials
  • hide: tinted primer reduces number of topcoats needed for dramatic color changes

Professionals often use dedicated primers — not paint‑and‑primer‑in‑one — for best results.

⚠️ common prep mistakes to avoid

  • painting over dirty or greasy walls
  • skipping sanding on glossy surfaces
  • not priming bare drywall or wood
  • using cheap caulk that cracks or won't take paint
  • rushing — letting patches or caulk dry fully is essential
